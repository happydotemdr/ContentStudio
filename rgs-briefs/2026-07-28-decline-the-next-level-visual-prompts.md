## Motion rationing — rationale per beat

Per the locked visual system, i2v is reserved for beats that genuinely need motion the edit
itself cannot fake — the Hook and the Turn/climax beat `[B]`. Applied here, beat by beat:

- **Hook (A-01) — motion.** The brand's own hook constraint says the first 2 seconds must carry
  the whole video visually *and* verbally, and the script's own visual note requires the form to
  be "already moving... mid-motion at 00:00," not a still frame of a hand reaching `[B]`. A static
  still cannot show the form *already in motion* at frame one — that requires an actual moving
  clip. Muted-autoplay load-bearing per the script's own flag `[C] (vidIQ, DiZnbihU4NM)`.
- **Setup (A-02) — still.** The VO names an object ("that offer... outside the playing"); the
  visual note describes a static tiered stack, not continuous action. A still plus edit-added
  push-in (per the corpus's slow-zoom premium-feel note `[C] (One Person Business, eVePkmCQV5c)`)
  covers it.
- **Build — proof (A-03/04/05) — stills.** The VO describes a research finding, not an action —
  the citation plate does the claim-carrying work `[C] (vidIQ, i5bZ-Be9cAQ)`, and three stills at
  the ~3s cadence deliver the "sampling childhood made physical" visual demonstration the script
  calls for without needing continuous motion.
- **Re-hook (A-06) — still.** A single focus-pull composition (gear blurred at the frame edge,
  letterhead sharp) — the *idea* of a subject change is carried by composition, not by an actual
  camera move; a 3-second beat at the cadence limit doesn't support more.
- **Build — Dewey (A-07) — motion, Short A's climax beat.** This is the beat both the grounding
  brief and the locked visual system single out as doing "the most work" `[I]` (visual system,
  Motion rationing) — the mechanism the whole Short turns on, landing as a verbatim 1916 quote.
  The visual note calls for a camera move onto the blank signature line as the moment lands; a
  static image can't sell a reveal, which is exactly tier 3 of the still-vs-motion decision table
  (`references/image-to-video.md`). This is also where the Hook's "already moving" symmetry pays
  off at the Loop — both the Short's alpha and near-omega beats carry real motion, its middle does
  not.
- **Payoff (A-08/09/10) — stills.** The VO argument ("the industry needs a next tier to sell") is
  reframe text, not action; three stills reading the tier stack as a receding ladder deliver the
  "no top visible" idea through composition and framing, which doesn't need motion to land.
- **Loop/CTA — reuse, no new asset.** Mirrors the Hook exactly, so whatever motion treatment A-01
  gets at edit time (the rendered i2v clip, held or looped) is what closes the Short too — no
  separate motion decision needed here.

## The Dewey quote-card readability problem — resolved, not passed along

**Inherited from `voiceover-brief`:** the 21–28s Build beat is a 7-second window carrying a
22-word verbatim on-screen quote card while the VO speaks a separate 20-word paraphrase. That is
the tightest read in the piece, and the upstream stage explicitly left the on-screen readability
question open for this stage.

**The arithmetic that makes it a real problem.** 22 words in 7 seconds is ~3.1 words/second of
*reading* — casual on-screen reading runs meaningfully slower than that once a reader also has to
parse a title-case attribution line underneath, at the safe zone's reduced usable height (middle
~60% vertical only, per the visual system's `[T-unverified]` working rule). A single static card
at that density risks the exact "dead air" failure the pacing rules warn about, just inverted:
not an unchanging *image*, but unreadable *text* on top of one `[C] (vidIQ, DiZnbihU4NM)`,
adapted to text density rather than image staleness.

**Resolution: split the card into two sequential reveals inside the same 7-second beat**, rather
than shortening the quote (already rejected upstream — see the script's Alternates §3, which
picked this exact 22-word line over a 38-word alternative specifically because it fits a 7-second
beat) or extending the beat (the beat-timing model's 45s total is fixed and every other beat is
load-bearing).

- **Reveal 1 (~21–24.5s, ~3.5s):** "it becomes constrained labor when the consequences are
  outside of the activity as an end" (12 words).
- **Reveal 2 (~24.5–28s, ~3.5s):** "to which activity is merely a means." — John Dewey,
  *Democracy and Education*, 1916 (10 words + attribution line).

Each half lands at ~3.4–3.5 words/second of *display* time budgeted for reading, not 3.1
words/second across the whole card at once — a meaningfully easier read at the same total word
count, and it keeps the caption-cadence promise the visual system already made ("burned-in,
revealed at beat boundaries... one caption per script beat") by treating each half as its own
sub-beat reveal rather than word-by-word karaoke `[B]`. Both halves stay inside the safe zone
(middle ~60% vertical, clear of the bottom 25% and right 15%) and both use the same single bold
sans-serif at the locked weight `[B][T-unverified]`.

**This is a caption-timing decision, not a new still.** A-07's single background (and its i2v
clip) supports two sequential text reveals without any new Midjourney generation or a second
still ID — Midjourney never renders this text anyway (every prompt ends `No Text.`), so the split
costs nothing at this stage. **Handed to `shorts-assembly` (Task 13) as a binding requirement**,
not a suggestion: implement the quote card as two reveals at the timings above, not one static
22-word card. `[I]` (this skill's own resolution — the corpus has no finding on quote-card word
density specifically; the reading-speed argument above is craft judgment, flagged as such rather
than presented as corpus-sourced).
