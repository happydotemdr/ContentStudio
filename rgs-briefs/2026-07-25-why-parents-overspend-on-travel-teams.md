# Grounding Brief: why parents overspend on travel teams

## Pairing
- **Thinker:** Thorstein Veblen, *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899) — invidious
  comparison [THINKER: Thorstein Veblen, The Theory of the Leisure Class, paraphrase-caution]
- **Research:** DeFreese & Dorsch 2018 / APA 2024, F4 (sport-parent burnout & overinvolvement)
  — chronic emotional/financial overinvestment without adequate recovery, a measurable burnout
  syndrome [RESEARCH: DeFreese & Dorsch 2018, Quality: Medium-High]
- **Why this pairing:** Veblen's mechanism is esteem gained through comparison-driven display
  relative to one's peers; F4's construct is chronic emotional/physical investment in a child's
  sport career without adequate recovery. The parallel: sport-parent overinvestment is
  plausibly sustained, in part, by exactly the status-comparison dynamic Veblen describes
  (keeping pace with other travel-team families) — an interpretive lens on *why* the investment
  becomes chronic, not a claim F4's cited studies measured Veblen's mechanism directly.

## Hook
Open on Veblen's 1899 line, paraphrased in voiceover (paraphrase-caution — do not put on a
quote card): people size up their own standing by comparing themselves to the people around
them, and that comparison, once habitual, never lets them feel like they've caught up — "the
invidious comparison can never become so favourable to the individual making it that he would
not gladly rate himself still higher relatively to his competitors" (Veblen, *Theory of the
Leisure Class*, Ch. II, "Pecuniary Emulation"). Land it on a travel-team sideline: the same
mechanism, just running on club fees and gear instead of Veblen's carriages and calling cards.

## Turn
Name it plainly: this isn't really about the kid's talent ceiling — it's the same "invidious
comparison" instinct Veblen described 125 years ago, now running on travel-ball budgets. The
system (the travel-team economy) supplies the scoreboard parents compare themselves on, the
same way Veblen's leisure class compared itself on visible consumption.

## Payoff
Use F4's own Content Hook rather than re-deriving a number: "There's a name for what happens to
parents who give everything to their kid's sport career: sport-parent burnout. It's real, it's
measurable, and it predicts worse outcomes for their kids." (DeFreese & Dorsch 2018, Journal of
Clinical Sport Psychology 12:218–233 — parent-child conflict in sport predicts higher burnout;
highly engaged parents are at risk of both higher social support AND higher burnout.) Frame as
association, not causation, per research-corpus-protocol.md's rule for F4 (observational data).
Do not use the "94% mothers" load figure or the "warmth + high expectations is protective"
reading — both were struck from this file's own record as unverifiable/inverted (see
cautionNote, `rgs-f4-sport-parent-burnout.md`).

## Reframe
It was never about your kid's talent — that's the good news. The spending pressure is the
system running a comparison game on you, the same instinct Veblen named in 1899. Naming it is
what breaks its grip: you can opt out of the comparison without opting out of your kid's sport.
