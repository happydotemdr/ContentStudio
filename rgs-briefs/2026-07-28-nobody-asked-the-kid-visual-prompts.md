## Shot count — how 50 seconds became 15 shots

The cadence rule is the one `[C]` line that sets shot count: change the on-screen visual roughly
every ~3 seconds and never hold one image too long, because a static unchanging frame is the visual
equivalent of dead air `[C] (Make Money Matt, HopTPCLbiiM; vidIQ, DiZnbihU4NM)`. Matching the visual
to what is being said sentence-by-sentence is the same rule's other half — a mismatched visual causes
confusion and drop-off `[C] (Kallaway, i7upRL4H1FM)`.

| Beat | Range | Duration | Shots | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3s | 3s | 1 | already at the cadence limit; one frame, animated |
| Setup | 3–8s | 5s | 2 | the present-day claim, then the register switch that opens 1886 |
| Build — Mason's claim | 8–14s | 6s | 2 | the ledger artifact, then the present-day counterpart |
| Re-hook | 14–17s | 3s | 1 | one frame; the focus shift *is* the beat |
| Build — quote card | 17–26s | 9s | 2 | **deliberate under-cut, see below** |
| Payoff — research | 26–38s | 12s | 3 | four would be the strict cadence read; three keeps each citation plate readable |
| Payoff — reframe | 38–45s | 7s | 2 | the sheet's columns, then the sheet abandoned |
| Loop/CTA | 45–50s | 5s | 2 | the 1886 close, then the focus-pulled mirror of the Hook |
| **Total** | **0–50s** | **50s** | **15** | |

**Two deliberate departures from the ~3s cadence, stated rather than averaged away** `[I]`:

1. **The quote-card beat (17–26s) gets 2 shots, not 3.** Strict cadence wants three. The script binds
   an 18-word verbatim Mason card to hold on screen long enough to be read, and the card is
   `shorts-assembly`'s overlay sitting *on top* of these stills. Cutting the plate beneath it three
   times in nine seconds fights the card's readability for no informational gain. Two 4.5-second
   holds is the compromise, and it is a knowing override of a `[C]` rule, not a reading of it.
2. **The research beat (26–38s) gets 3 shots, not 4.** Same reason: citation plate 1 holds from 26s
   and figure plate 2 is revealed ~31s and held to 38s. The stills beneath change on the plate
   boundaries rather than on a metronome. The corpus's own counter-caution supports the direction —
   over-editing exhausts viewers and comprehension comes from *deleting* edits
   `[C] (Kallaway, i7upRL4H1FM; Nate Black, J8LrrCpDNJI)`.

**How the script's "must render as on-screen text, not B-roll" flag is honored** `[I]`. The script
flags three beats — the Mason quote card (17–26s) and both research plates (26–38s) — as carrying
claims that must appear as on-screen text rather than being left to B-roll
`[C] (vidIQ, i5bZ-Be9cAQ)`. That flag binds the **overlay**, not the underlying image: Midjourney
cannot reliably render legible text `[C] (Tokenized AI, qFYJb0zYztY)`, so every prompt on this sheet
ends `No Text.` and every word of copy is listed in the OVERLAY COPY HANDOFF block for
`shorts-assembly` to composite. The stills under those overlays still have to be *something*, and
what they are is specified below. Reading the script's flag as "generate no image for these beats"
would leave three beats with nothing behind the text.

prevents is real and documented — a prior production sheet rendered six of nine stills as the same
photograph because prompts were written one beat at a time by cloning the previous body and swapping

**By-eye check run against `visual-arc.md` §4–§7 before any prompt was written** `[I]`:

- **Scales:** `CLOSE, MID-WIDE, WIDE, MACRO, MID, XWIDE` — 6 distinct (C4 wants ≥3), no two
  consecutive rows repeat one (C2).
- **Camera heights:** `EYE, LOW, OVERHEAD, HIGH` — 4 distinct (C5 wants ≥2).
- **Shot classes:** no two consecutive rows repeat one (C1). Register A visits all four of its
  classes; Register B visits all three of its. Nothing leans on a single class — and specifically
  the sheet is not built out of `HUMAN-COST` shots, which is the taxonomy's named failure mode.
- **Register rhythm:** `A A B B A A B B A A B A A B A` — no run longer than 2 (C3), 9 alternations
  (C7 wants ≥2), 9 Register A and 6 Register B (C6 wants ≥3 and ≥2).
- **The two script-binding constraints are satisfied at the table, not patched into prompts later:**
  the reframe rows (12, 13) carry **no parent figure and no child figure**, per the script's binding
  note that the villain there is the structure and not a person `[B]`; and the Loop/CTA row (15) is
  the focus-pulled variant of row 1 rather than a cut back to the identical frame, which is Short B's
  answer to Short A's exact-shot loop — the same loop mechanic, differently executed, so the pair
  reads as companions rather than one template refilled `[I]`.

**One taxonomy caveat, flagged rather than glossed** `[I]`: rows 5, 10 and 15 are typed
`HUMAN-COST`, which is the register's name for *the emotional weight of the claim carried on a body
rather than a wide shot*. On this Short that weight is **warmth**, not cost — a child mid-telling, a
child mid-effort, a child laughing. The class name describes the composition, not the emotion, and
using it here does not license a distress register. The brand's binding rule is that the child's
answer is written warm, and drift toward distress is a rework trigger, not a stylistic option `[B]`.

## Reuse marking — and why nothing is reused

Per the locked visual system's still-pool protocol, **every** shot on this sheet carries exactly one
of `REUSE <A-NN>` or `NEW <B-NN>`; there is no third state `[I]`. The result of applying that
protocol honestly is that **zero of Short A's eleven stills are reused.** Every shot below is
`NEW`. That is the expected outcome under the visual system as amended 2026-07-28, not a defect —
see the Asset economy section for the arithmetic and the plain statement of the missed target.

**Reuse was tested against the pool and rejected, ID by ID** `[I]`. The candidates were the only
three with any surface overlap:

| Candidate | Why it was rejected |
|---|---|
| `A-03` (soccer cleats and shin guards on a dark wooden table) | The one Short A still that names soccer at all. It carries none of this Short's `register_a_signature_objects` and is staged on an indoor table, not the locked `municipal youth soccer ground` — so it fires Gate C's **C8** on its own world lock, and it imports motif family A (gear laid out as objects) into a Short whose whole grammar is a two-plane depth composition. Forcing it in would damage the beat it landed on. |
| `A-07` (blank unsigned form, capped pen — Short A's Dewey climax) | Register A by construction, but its subject is *an offer waiting to be signed* — Short A's argument, not Short B's. Short B's climax beat is 1886 and Register B; there is no beat here for it to serve. |
| `A-11` (staged cover/thumbnail) | Short A's thumbnail is a hand pushing a registration form on a single plane. Short B's packaging is a two-plane depth composition with a left-third text zone and a binding 120px squint-test fix. Different composition, different claim type ("you're allowed" vs. "it's not on here"). |

Two structural reasons sit underneath all three rejections, both verified rather than assumed
`[I]`: Short A's pool `A-01`…`A-11` is **entirely Register A**, so a Short B sheet drawn from it
cannot reach Gate C's `C6`/`C7` minimums no matter how it is worded; and Short A's sheet names three
sports against the one-sport world lock and does not parse under `scripts/lint_prompt_sheet.py` at
all. Its stills are not a compliant source pool. **No REUSE was faked to satisfy an assertion** —
the marking protocol is enforced, the reuse floor is not, and the difference is stated here rather
than hidden in the count.
