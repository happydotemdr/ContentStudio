# Concept Brief: The Ancient Name for What's Buying Up Youth Sports

## Angle / take
For parents already inside the travel/club sports fee system, this Short names the actual
economic mechanism behind rising costs — using Aristotle's 2,300-year-old distinction between
wealth-getting that serves real use and "unnatural" wealth-getting that exists only to make more
money (usury, monopoly-cornering) — with the newly-defined scope of a pending federal consumer-
protection proposal as the concrete, present-day evidence that it's happening to the platforms
families already use.
Derived as a format-collision "purple cow": RGS's signature "ancient thinker predicts modern
problem" pattern, applied for the first time to a breaking-news economic/consumer angle instead
of the channel's usual burnout/specialization/pressure topics — differentiated from both (a)
political/legislative-news coverage of the underlying bill and (b) generic "youth sports costs
too much" complaint content, neither of which explains the *mechanism*, just the symptom.

## Hook concept
- Emotional trigger: curiosity (an information-gap "there's an old word for this" hook), with
  the "Access / Inside X" title frame (the registration app on a parent's own phone as the
  "inside" object)
- The promise: the opening must deliver a concrete, plain-language naming of the economic
  mechanism (buying control of something you didn't build, then charging rent on access to it)
  within the first beat — not a bill summary, not a political update, and not vague outrage.

## Validation
- **Net information gain:** Existing coverage of this bill is either partisan news framing
  (naming sponsors, "Democrats introduce...") or advocacy-group stat-repackaging (the "$40B
  industry" / "$5,000+" figures). This idea adds neither — it names the underlying economic
  mechanism via a 2,300-year-old economic distinction and uses the bill's own definitional
  scope (registration/scheduling platforms counted as "youth sports") as evidence, giving
  viewers a durable pattern-recognition tool rather than a news update. Pass.
- **Home-feed click test:** Pass — an ancient-thinker-names-a-modern-scam hook paired with a
  concrete, relatable object (the app on your own phone) earns a cold click; this is RGS's
  already-proven signature format, applied to a fresh subject.
- **Packaging-compellingness:** Pass — specific avatar (parents currently paying club/travel
  fees), one clear thumbnail focal point, thumbnail complements rather than repeats the title.
- **Demonetization/policy screen: FLAGGED — mitigated, not clear by default.** The underlying
  topic is a live federal bill with named partisan sponsors; the corpus flags political content
  on a faceless channel as a ~99.9% termination-risk category. This idea only clears the screen
  under a binding constraint that must carry forward to scripting: **no sponsor names, no party
  references, no "Congress"/"Senate"/committee-status/political-horse-race framing anywhere in
  title, thumbnail, or script.** The bill functions only as an unnamed "a newly proposed federal
  law would..." evidentiary beat for the mechanism — the video's subject is the economic
  pattern and the ancient naming of it, never the legislation's politics or its odds of passing.
  Do not discuss committee status, the partisan cosponsor list, or "challenging path" framing on
  screen — those belong to the grounding brief's internal citation record only, not the Short.
