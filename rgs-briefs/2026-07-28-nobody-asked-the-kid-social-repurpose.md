## A3 check — does any line read as an accusation of the parent?

The archetype's specific risk is that copy written from inside the child's experience
slides into implying the parent is the one at fault `[B]`. Every surface was read for it.

| Risk | Where it could appear | How the copy handles it |
|---|---|---|
| The child's quoted line reading as a complaint | all six surfaces open on it | It is a laughing memory with no addressee, no absence named, and no adult in the sentence — the shipped Hook precisely because the rejected alternate ("nobody asked me…") made the viewer the grammatical target `[B]`; script, Alternates 1. |
| Mason's "our contempt" heard as "your contempt" | YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Bluesky, Threads | The date **1886** appears in the same sentence as the quote on every surface that carries it, and the quote is always framed as Mason's words before it is spoken. The script's third mitigation — the "It was never yours" clause — lives in the video, not the copy; the date-in-sentence construction is the copy's equivalent `[I]`. |
| The research heard as a rebuke of parents who care about winning | all surfaces | Every surface reports what children *named*. No surface says winning harms anyone, and none converts group data into a claim about the reader's own child — the unpublishable form is absent everywhere `[RESEARCH: Visek et al. 2015]` `[B]`. |
| "Believe the answer" heard as "you don't believe your kid" | YouTube, Instagram | Both surfaces place it after the exoneration line, so the disbelief is already assigned to a 140-year-old reflex and disowned on the reader's behalf `[B]`. The two surfaces that could not carry the full sequence (X, Bluesky) **drop the line rather than stranding it** — a bare "believe the answer" with no exoneration in front of it is the accusation `[I]`. |
| The parent as agent rather than recipient | the reframe line, four surfaces | The grammar is unchanged from the script: "you *were handed* a standings sheet." The parent is the object of a bad instrument, never the author of one `[B]`. |
