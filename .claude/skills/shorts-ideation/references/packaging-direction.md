# Title & thumbnail packaging direction

Distilled from `docs/headless-youtube-audit.md` §7 (Packaging: titles/thumbnails/CTR).
This file covers *direction* — the shape, frame, and constraints for the title and
thumbnail — not the final rendered thumbnail image or the fully-polished title (that's
downstream production work once a script exists). The concept brief should hand
`shorts-scripting` (and eventually the assembly/social stages) a clear packaging direction
to build toward, in the same way `hook-concepts.md` hands over a promise to keep.

## Packaging outranks production quality

- **`[C]` Packaging outranks production quality — strongly-supported.** A 2-hour video can
  outperform a 20-hour one because viewers can't judge quality until they click; packaging
  earns the click, the finished video earns the subscribe (Roberto Blake, InN8JOCRyXI;
  Roberto Blake, 4uSOnI0v8U0).
- **`[C]` Decide the title and thumbnail before committing to the video — strongly-implied
  by the corpus's repeated framing.** If you can't make compelling packaging, the idea
  itself is weak; deciding packaging first lets you shoot specifically to deliver on it
  (Nick Nimmin, kcSOFqJhR9I; Romayroh, 1UkLWW0bs7o). This is why packaging direction is part
  of the ideation-stage concept brief rather than a later production decision.

## Title mechanics

- **`[C]` Keep titles short — roughly 40–60 characters — and front-load the strong words —
  strongly-supported.** Titles truncate at ~55 characters and 70%+ of views are mobile;
  top-performer distributions cluster 40–50 characters (Dan the creator, CWcalhl86DE; Nick
  Nimmin, LAzYEKltBwA; Roberto Blake, InN8JOCRyXI [CLICKS method]; Nate Black,
  9mLuaqqW1jY). ~81.6% of top titles scored maximum clarity — unclear titles don't get
  clicked by new viewers (Nate Black, 9mLuaqqW1jY).
- **`[C]` Be specific and call out the avatar — strongly-supported.** Vague, broad titles
  fail for small channels; naming "swollen ankles after 60" or "Gen X men" lets the
  algorithm find the right viewer (One Person Business, MP7JYOm25-g; Nick Nimmin,
  LAzYEKltBwA; vidIQ, SIp7MeYbz8U). Specificity makes the outcome feel attainable ("I made
  this blanket in just 3 days"). Vague packaging that works for a 4M-sub channel fails on a
  small channel, which needs value-explicit packaging (Nate Black, DNQ4YIkNBms).
- **`[C]` Read the title out loud to check it sounds conversational** (Dan the creator,
  v562jH_TESg). When generating title options with AI, request 10 titles under 60
  characters, avoid colons/em-dashes/semicolons, and pair each with a matching thumbnail
  concept (Nick Nimmin, LCU3tjJHOaY) — but see `angle-selection.md`'s rule against asking AI
  for the raw idea itself; this is packaging polish, not idea generation.

## Thumbnail direction

- **`[C]` One clear focal point, readable at a glance ("highway billboard") —
  strongly-supported.** Viewers give a thumbnail ~1 second; make the focus the largest
  object, minimal text (3–6 words max), high contrast, one emotional signal (Nick Nimmin,
  h8OTdq24irE; Nick Nimmin, LAzYEKltBwA; One Person Business, eVePkmCQV5c; Dan the creator,
  TY4cm66X_oY). A face is not required.
- **`[C]` The two-brain model:** the emotional (right) brain reacts first to faces, one
  dominant emotion, contrast, and pattern interrupts to stop the scroll; the logical (left)
  brain then checks clarity, credibility, expected payoff, and title alignment to decide the
  click (Dan the creator, TY4cm66X_oY). Use at most one dominant emotion plus one supporting
  trigger — two competing emotions cancel out. A big claim from a tiny, unproven channel
  fails on credibility (Dan the creator, TY4cm66X_oY).
- **`[C]` Roberto Blake's VIBES framework** — visually attractive, interesting idea, bold
  colors/text, eye-catching elements, status/social proof — at most three eye-catching
  elements (Roberto Blake, InN8JOCRyXI).
- **`[C]` Thumbnail should complement, not repeat, the title — strongly-supported.** You
  only get two things to earn a click; repeating the title's words wastes the space, so the
  thumbnail should hit a *different* emotional trigger and create a loop only clickable to
  close (One Person Business, 84bavOadYCI; One Person Business, MP7JYOm25-g). Don't put the
  channel name/logo in the thumbnail — it wastes the 3–5 words available (One Person
  Business, 6RSWJ0IxKvs). Show the result/consequence without explaining it (a clear
  number, not a laptop with dollar signs); don't give away the whole answer or use jargon
  that slows comprehension (One Person Business, eVePkmCQV5c; One Person Business,
  84bavOadYCI).
- **`[C]` Copy the thumbnail FORMAT of the niche, not any specific thumbnail** — a white
  rectangle/black text/arrow style applied to your own unique item is "original in its own
  way"; copying an actual existing thumbnail design risks termination (Romayroh,
  KHhUwdFXKyA). Hold the direction to the bar of one that already gets ~100k views in the
  niche (Roberto Blake, InN8JOCRyXI).
- **`[C]` The "un-thumbnail" counter-pattern for small channels.** 72% of standout
  small-channel videos in Nate Black's study used "un-thumbnails": an original (often
  staged) photo, subtle color/light correction, 2–4 bold words, optional familiar symbols —
  simpler than a highly-engineered thumbnail, which can push small creators into a cluttered
  "dead zone" that underperforms a raw one (Nate Black, -zd1lLaC-I0). Minimalist "less is
  more" packaging with deliberate dead space can outperform busy packaging (Nate Black,
  acOx8xUNXyQ).

## Shorts-specific constraints

- **`[C]` Shorts allow only one title and one thumbnail — no A/B test — but custom Shorts
  thumbnails now exist and matter when Shorts traffic comes from search.** Decide the
  packaging direction with the knowledge that it won't be testable post-publish the way a
  long-form video's would (vidIQ, mgqkfDBT7gU). This raises the stakes on getting the
  direction right at the concept-brief stage rather than iterating after upload.

## Vetting the direction

- **`[C]` CTR is the master packaging metric; sub-5% (2–4% is terrible) is a signal to
  revisit title/thumbnail — strongly-supported** (One Person Business, MP7JYOm25-g;
  Romayroh, 1UkLWW0bs7o). This is a post-publish check, not a pre-publish gate — flag it in
  the concept brief as the metric to watch, not something to satisfy before scripting
  begins (the pre-publish gate is in `validation-gate.md`).
