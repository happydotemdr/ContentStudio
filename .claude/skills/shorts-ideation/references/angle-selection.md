# Angle & take selection

Distilled from `docs/headless-youtube-audit.md` §1 (Niche selection & validation) and §3
(Ideation & content strategy). Every rule below carries its corpus marker; `[C]` lines
preserve `(Channel, video_id)` citations exactly as the audit gives them. "Strongly-supported"
means two or more channels independently corroborated the point — carried through from the
audit verbatim.

Use this file when narrowing a raw idea down to one specific angle/take. Read
`hook-concepts.md` next to shape how that angle becomes a hook, and `packaging-direction.md`
for title/thumbnail direction.

## The idea is the root lever

- **`[C]` Most video failures are idea failures, not hook/edit failures — strongly-supported.**
  When videos fail, the problem is almost always the topic or the take, not the
  hook/storytelling/editing (Kallaway, i7upRL4H1FM; vidIQ, AMlVWoRNszc). A small channel's
  real advantage is the creative freedom to pull the better-idea lever faster than a giant
  channel can (vidIQ, AMlVWoRNszc).
- **`[C]` Every video needs a clear value proposition before you commit to it.** Ask "what
  will a viewer gain?" before recording; if you can't answer, neither can the viewer
  (Dan the creator, 9JE8-wM8zKc). "What I wish I knew before backpacking in Bali" beats
  "How to travel Bali" (Dan the creator, bTr-Izh9pkc).
- **`[C]` Every video must deliver education, entertainment, or information — strongly-supported**
  (Dan the creator, 9JE8-wM8zKc).
- **`[C]` The "edutainment wrap": wrap the lesson in a story/challenge so it teaches the same
  thing with stakes** — "I ran a marathon with no experience" teaches the same lesson as a
  plain how-to, but the stakes make it watchable (Dan the creator, 8sLWWv_EAR4).

## Niche down to one specific viewer avatar

- **`[C]` Niche down to one specific viewer avatar, not a broad topic — strongly-supported.**
  A specific avatar ("psychology of Gen X men," "personal finance for people approaching
  retirement") lets the algorithm instantly identify the audience; broad titles force it to
  guess and tank CTR (One Person Business, MP7JYOm25-g; Make Money Matt, n7KG9AvdB-s;
  Kallaway, DFSU-YPRTs8; Nick Nimmin, kcSOFqJhR9I; Dan the creator, To5Dyc7AbkI; Roberto
  Blake, TOx0RmdH1q4). Understand *why* the viewer watches, not just the topic — two people
  watch the same travel video for different reasons (Dan the creator, bTr-Izh9pkc).
- **`[C]` Don't over-narrow to where the addressable audience is tiny** — e.g. Canon users
  new to wedding photography who won't spend $300 (Nick Nimmin, la031ela7Sw); beware a
  hyper-specific "one-to-one" ceiling, like a build-along for one exact kit that only serves
  people building that kit (vidIQ, g844t-iFzxA).

## Be different, not better

- **`[C]` "Better" is invisible against an established channel — be different instead (the
  "purple cow").** If YouTube offers your video versus the same topic from a bigger channel,
  the bigger channel wins almost every time (vidIQ, G1erIiTByPQ). Four repeatable patterns,
  in order of durability:
  1. **Format collision** — merge two unrelated formats (One Punch Man + fitness).
  2. **Alternative context** — reframe the topic inside an unrelated world (biology as an MMO).
  3. **Commitment premise** — a multi-year finite project; the most copy-proof pattern.
  4. **Improbable source** — an unexpected voice on the topic (a poetry professor analyzing
     Kendrick).
  (vidIQ, G1erIiTByPQ)
- **`[C]` Raise the barrier to entry** — if a 15-year-old could make your video, the market is
  saturated for you (Roberto Blake, 4uSOnI0v8U0).

## Import, remix, combine — don't invent from a blank page

- **`[C]` Niche-bending / niche-quilting / transpositioning: import a proven format from
  another niche before anyone else does — strongly-supported.** Copying within your own
  niche no longer works; importing an outside format ("I tried X for 30 days," tier lists,
  "explained in X minutes") feels novel and gives a proven idea at low competition (Dan the
  creator, oWUG6gwWrME; Nick Nimmin, h8OTdq24irE; Kallaway, ceRZVxO8KF8; One Person
  Business, Dk5r8thm1Ws; vidIQ, 9z1ACpWW9do). Keep niche-bent videos to ~20% of output so you
  don't confuse your channel identity, and never bend a format from a niche you don't
  understand (Dan the creator, oWUG6gwWrME).
- **`[C]` Hunt outlier videos, not big creators — strongly-supported.** Find videos
  overperforming their own channel's baseline in the last 90 days and build on the patterns
  they haven't fully committed to (Roberto Blake, InN8JOCRyXI [FOCUS framework]; One Person
  Business, s2knfD7QuCM; vidIQ, 9z1ACpWW9do; vidIQ, AMlVWoRNszc). Study small-to-medium
  creators (~10k–1M, even nano 5k–50k) rather than mega accounts — big accounts compress
  performance because even weak videos do fine on trust, hiding what actually drove the
  result ("Kallaway's paradox") (Kallaway, o_n5ED5fE1I; Kallaway, pS2Mi1IQI-c; Dan the
  creator, Wx5gF0jkgZI).
- **`[C]` Combine two independently-proven ideas — strongly-supported.** Merge "illegal side
  hustles" + "niches to avoid" into "niches banned on YouTube"; screenshot a channel's top
  videos and have Claude return combined ideas ranked best to worst (Make Money Matt,
  xZfM93XGy0g; One Person Business, eVePkmCQV5c).
- **`[C]` Mine comments for the real audience — strongly-supported.** Reading ~100 comments
  on top videos in the niche surfaces the same repeated questions and frustrations;
  answering them makes viewers feel understood and spikes retention (One Person Business,
  eVePkmCQV5c; Make Money Matt, xZfM93XGy0g). Extract the viewer avatar and channel promise
  from comment data, not analytics — analytics only report account-holder age (vidIQ,
  C3GGi8fUvBA).
- **`[C]` Study a competitor, then change the angle — never copy — strongly-supported.**
  Same format, transferred angle: "10 fruits" instead of "10 vegetables," knee pain instead
  of back pain (Romayroh, OrPYWlXMQws; vidIQ, 9z1ACpWW9do). Don't copy verbatim — YouTube's
  redundancy filter suppresses near-duplicate content, excluding it from the recommendation
  pool (Romayroh, KHhUwdFXKyA).

## Keep the take human

- **`[C]` Keep the ideas human, delegate the rest — strongly-supported.** Never ask
  ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini for raw video ideas or titles — they lack real YouTube performance
  data and produce generic, interchangeable output; feed an AI assistant a real outlier's
  title structure and adapt only 1–3 words, or use it for topic *research* instead (One
  Person Business, 84bavOadYCI; Romayroh, OrPYWlXMQws; Nate Black, J8LrrCpDNJI). You can
  delegate editing, scripting, voiceover, and thumbnails — but the idea must be yours
  (Romayroh, OrPYWlXMQws).
- **`[C]` Build every video around a contrarian take.** State the common belief, introduce
  your contrarian frame, then explain why yours is right; validate the topic with data but
  keep the take creative (Kallaway, 6G1wIdxWF5w; Kallaway, 46t_cn0lx2E). Content only builds
  trust when it's both non-obvious *and* tactically implementable (Kallaway, 8cQidXgtGmU).
  Creativity is the collision of two things that don't usually go together — consume outside
  your niche for raw material (Kallaway, cuVyTmbOZjk).
- **`[C]` Kallaway's "360 mapping": list every possible take on an idea and score each for
  shock value, to surface the most differentiated angle before you commit** (Kallaway,
  i7upRL4H1FM).
- **`[C]` Compete with AI answer engines via personality and originality — strongly-supported.**
  Basic educational demand is being absorbed by ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude; YouTube's edge is the
  human element (Dan the creator, 4GAKrgNN8zQ). Find your "spark angle" — content only you
  could make. Generic, low-preference "how to change a tire" content is most exposed to AI
  replacement; personality/artistry-driven content is defensible (Nate Black, 7KL_zwHd2yM;
  Nate Black, e_QqUfD-HYU; Nate Black, J8LrrCpDNJI).

## Idea-shape levers & guardrails

- **`[C]` The "Trojan horse": package niche/educational content inside a mass-appeal shell.**
  Mark Rober's squirrel-obstacle-course video delivers an engineering lesson inside a mass-appeal
  animal-antics shell; "how does Bitcoin actually work" does the same for a niche financial topic
  (vidIQ, VLfzk9NlZyI). The same idea appears as an idea-vetting step — pick a mass-appeal shell
  over the niche core before committing to the idea (docs/headless-shorts-production-playbook.md,
  vidIQ, VLfzk9NlZyI).
- **`[C]` Target unfilled, intersectional concept slots rather than saturated searches**
  ("10 goats to solve my thistle problem"); absurd ideas turn the audience into informants
  who share for you (Nate Black, acOx8xUNXyQ). Use the "audience bridge": reframe a narrow
  topic around the viewer who benefits ("this mouse doubled my productivity," not "Nimmin
  Mouse 2000 review") (Nick Nimmin, LAzYEKltBwA). A video's ceiling is the number of people
  interested in the topic — treat topic choice as a numbers game (Nick Nimmin, LAzYEKltBwA).
- **`[C]` Avoid the demonetization-magnet niches.** Fully AI-generated "AI stories" (1–10
  static images over an hour), sleeping, religion, motivation, and spirituality are top
  targets for inauthentic-content strikes (Romayroh, KbUXzJ55eJk). Never do political
  content on a faceless channel — ~99.9% termination risk claimed (Romayroh, Mei39lsp9BE).
  Avoid sensitive medical/financial advice and politicians'/celebrities' likenesses in
  thumbnails (One Person Business, rQQjAWmA-9c).
