# Hook concept

**Scope note:** this file covers the hook *concept* — which curiosity gap, emotional
trigger, or title frame the packaging promises, and what promise the opening must deliver
on. It does **not** cover hook *execution* — exact opening lines, in-medias-res staging,
re-hook cadence, contrast-word mechanics, or the 2-1-3 point ordering. Those are scripting
craft (`docs/headless-youtube-audit.md` §4) and belong to the downstream `shorts-scripting`
skill. Handing scripting a hook *concept* rather than a written hook keeps the two skills
atomic — don't let this file's output turn into a written opening.

Distilled from `docs/headless-youtube-audit.md` §7 (Packaging: titles) and §3 (Ideation,
contrarian-take framing), plus the "promise-breaker" pitfall from the Top-12 pitfalls list.

## Choose the emotional trigger

- **`[C]` Titles trigger emotion/curiosity, don't describe — via curiosity, fear, or desire
  — strongly-supported.** Curiosity opens an information gap ("This one mistake killed my
  channel"); fear leverages loss aversion; desire promises transformation (Dan the creator,
  CWcalhl86DE; One Person Business, MP7JYOm25-g; Roberto Blake, TOx0RmdH1q4 [five C's:
  curiosity, conflict, conspiracy, confusion, controversy]). Combining curiosity with
  negativity maximizes CTR (One Person Business, eVePkmCQV5c).
- **`[C]` Build the hook concept around a contrarian take.** State the common belief the
  viewer holds, name your contrarian frame, and note (for the scripting stage) why it's
  right — validate the topic with data but keep the take itself creative (Kallaway,
  6G1wIdxWF5w; Kallaway, 46t_cn0lx2E).
- **`[C]` Browse titles and search titles are different games.** Browse-surfaced Shorts lean
  on curiosity/emotion; search-surfaced content needs to be clear and keyword-focused
  instead (Dan the creator, CWcalhl86DE). Decide up front which game this idea is playing —
  it changes which trigger to lean on.

## Pick a title frame (2026 lift data, ~4M videos)

`[C]` (vidIQ, n5NER9cbueY) — use this frame data to decide the *shape* of the hook concept,
not to write the final title (packaging-direction.md covers title mechanics):

| Frame | Lift | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Witness / "caught on camera" | 4.41x | Highest lift in the dataset |
| Transformation / "turning X into Y" | 3.22x | |
| Access / "Inside X" | ~2.5x | Most versatile of the high-lift frames |
| Warning / "stop / don't / never" | ~1.77–2x | Works via loss aversion |
| Age / tenure credibility ("after 35 years") | ~2.05x | |
| Plain how-to | ~1.07 | Baseline, not a hook advantage |
| Question titles | ~0.82 | ~18% below average — underperforming |
| "I was wrong" confessions | <0.5x average | Underperforming |
| "Everything you know about X is wrong" | Burned out | Underperforming from overuse |

**`[C]` The biggest outliers stack 3–4 frames in one line** — "99-year-old abandoned cruise
ship transformed into a luxury ocean mansion" stacks duration + forbidden + transformation +
superlative for 12M views (vidIQ, n5NER9cbueY). Frames amplify a viable topic; they don't
fix a weak one — the angle from `angle-selection.md` has to earn the click on its own
merits first.

**`[C]` Revenue-title data (tracked across ~$17.7M in creator sales):** "How I" beats
"How to" — how-to pulls more clicks (32%) but far less revenue (18%) because it hands over
the solution and the viewer no longer needs the creator; exact dollar amounts, ideally as a
rate, pull 34% of clicks and 52% of revenue (One Person Business, iwnUyE_s-6E). Pair
hyper-specific odd numbers with identity warnings — "The $8,000 rule" beats "$10,000"
(vidIQ, Sgav7Bkg36M). Weight this data when the idea has a monetization angle; weight pure
curiosity/emotion triggers otherwise.

## The promise the hook concept must keep

- **`[C]` The first seconds must deliver on the packaging promise — strongly-supported.**
  A mismatch between the packaging (title/thumbnail) and how the video opens — the
  "promise-breaker" — makes viewers bounce and the algorithm reads it as the video not
  delivering (Dan the creator, 1_9Xq0QnDAw; One Person Business, 6RSWJ0IxKvs; Nick Nimmin,
  kcSOFqJhR9I). This is Top-12 pitfall #5 in the audit (One Person Business, 6RSWJ0IxKvs).
  At the ideation stage this means: **write down, in one sentence, the exact promise the
  hook concept makes** — that sentence is what `shorts-scripting` must open by delivering
  on. Don't let the concept brief hand scripting a vague or unresolved promise.
