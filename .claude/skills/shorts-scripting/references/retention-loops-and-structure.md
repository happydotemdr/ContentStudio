# Retention loops, the build, and the loop-to-start

Distilled from `docs/headless-youtube-audit.md` §4 ("Retention loops & structure")
and `docs/headless-shorts-production-playbook.md` §2 (single-idea rule, curiosity
loops, re-hooking, seamless loop). This covers the Setup → Build/Value → re-hook
portion of the beat model, plus the Loop/CTA beat's job of closing the circle.

## Re-hook on a timer — the core retention mechanic

- **Re-hook on a timer; open the next loop before the last closes — strongly- `[C]`
  supported.** The "tension engine" / hook-deliver cycle: open a curiosity loop,
  add value, resolve it, immediately open another — every 20–30 seconds on
  short-form `[C] (Make Money Matt, xZfM93XGy0g; Kallaway, pcnrzBwoVUk; Nate
  Black, c6X-Ywy3yVU; vidIQ, DiZnbihU4NM; Roberto Blake, q64Iczdhb-Y; One
  Person Business, eVePkmCQV5c)`. Stacking open loops keeps the viewer always
  one unanswered question ahead so there's nowhere to drift `[C] (vidIQ,
  DiZnbihU4NM)`.
- **Pair every resolution with a new hook — re-hook repeatedly, not only at the `[C]`
  intro.** `[C] (Nate Black, c6X-Ywy3yVU)` On a Short this concretely means a
  second curiosity beat around the **~15s mark**, right when the opening loop
  starts to feel answered — that specific placement is an inference from the
  general re-hook cadence, not a stated rule, so it's marked `[I]` in the beat
  timing model even though the underlying re-hook principle is `[C]`.
- **Continuously open new curiosity loops ("mystery boxes")** `[C]` so every stretch
  compels the next `[C] (Roberto Blake, q64Iczdhb-Y)`.
- **Plant an open curiosity loop early** `[C]` — promise a specific result revealed
  only at the end `[C] (Jenny Hoyos, oVKBAMEqsPI)` — medium confidence
  (Jenny Hoyos structural observation).

## Contrast is the engine

- **Contrast is the engine of curiosity; "but" is its most powerful word** `[C]` —
  contrast words (but, actually, instead, turns out, except) lean the viewer one
  way then flip them `[C] (Kallaway, pcnrzBwoVUk)`. Related: the **"but/so"
  framework** — "but" introduces conflict, "so" resolves it, chained like a
  movie `[C] (Nick Nimmin, LAzYEKltBwA)`.
- **Dopamine is a prediction chemical** `[C]`: run stakes → big question → head fake (a
  surprise that's unpredicted yet instantly logical) → re-hook `[C] (Kallaway,
  KyC8r-zitVE)`.

## Building the value beat

- **The biggest mid-video drop is caused by front-loaded setup and context, not `[C]`
  too much detail** `[C] (vidIQ, DiZnbihU4NM)` — get to the actual value fast;
  don't let Setup run long trying to be thorough.
- **Keep constant forward motion — rapid segment-to-segment transitions, no `[C]`
  dead air** `[C] (Jenny Hoyos, oVKBAMEqsPI)` — medium confidence — **but do
  not chase wall-to-wall stimulation and constant jump cuts to fake retention**
  `[C] (vidIQ, DiZnbihU4NM)`. These two sit in tension; resolve it by keeping the
  *script* tight (no wasted words) while leaving the *edit* decisions about cut
  frequency to the downstream assembly stage.
- **Compilation/escalation structures retain well** `[C]` because they inherently
  stack hook→deliver, setup→payoff cycles `[C] (Nate Black, c6X-Ywy3yVU)`;
  structure the Build as **escalation from small to progressively bigger
  steps** `[C] (Jenny Hoyos, TmtHoTrL-J8)` — medium confidence.
- **Consider a mid-Build twist that subverts the premise's expectation** `[C]
  (Jenny Hoyos, oVKBAMEqsPI)` — medium confidence.
- **Don't lead with your best point; build rising value (the "2-1-3" order)** `[C]` —
  the brain reads an upward sequence as a reason to stay; opening with the
  strongest point makes the rest feel like declining value `[C] (Kallaway,
  z8j3PupS5gc; One Person Business, MP7JYOm25-g)`. `[I]` This rule is documented
  for multi-point/list long-form videos; on a single-premise Short there is
  usually only one point, so apply the *spirit* of it (don't spend the best
  beat first and coast) rather than the literal 2-1-3 list ordering — this
  adaptation to single-premise Shorts is this skill's own synthesis.
- **Maintain high proof density** `[C]` — every unbacked claim tends to drop
  retention `[C] (One Person Business, eVePkmCQV5c)`. The corpus's cadence
  (every 30–60s) is a long-form number; on a 35–45s Short this compresses to
  **at least one concrete proof beat inside Build/Value** `[I]` (a number, a
  before/after, a named result) rather than a pure assertion.
- **Don't over-explain a simple concept** `[C]` — dragging out one point causes
  cognitive overload and click-off `[C] (Dan the creator, krgVTbNWrvU)`.
- **Use short sentences and deliberate half-second pauses** `[C]` so the key word
  lands `[C] (Kallaway, ZM3elcBE48I)`.
- **State claims with certain language** `[C]` ("when," "the reason why") **not
  hedges** ("if," "maybe") which create exit doors `[C] (Kallaway,
  pcnrzBwoVUk)`.
- **Use thought-narration** `[C]` ("you're probably thinking X") to simulate a
  one-on-one `[C] (Kallaway, pcnrzBwoVUk)`, and **singular "you," not "hey
  guys"** `[C] (Nick Nimmin, IF-PD6XMjYY)`.
- **Invert positive advice into a loss-averse warning** `[C]` ("this is killing your
  growth") where the premise allows it `[C] (Kallaway, pcnrzBwoVUk)`.
- **Name your frameworks (term branding)** `[C]` to create stickiness, if the Short's
  premise produces a repeatable technique worth naming `[C] (Kallaway,
  pcnrzBwoVUk)`.

## The seamless loop-to-start (Shorts-specific payoff mechanic)

- **Mirror the opening hook question in the closing line so the video forms a `[C]`
  satisfying loop** `[C] (Jenny Hoyos, mhVDcqnxxaY)` — medium confidence. On
  Shorts this makes the replay seamless: the Loop/CTA beat should echo the
  Hook's exact framing or key phrase, not just gesture at the topic.
- **Resolve on a climactic reveal that directly answers the hook's question** `[C]`
  `[C] (Jenny Hoyos, fukTZ82O4TU)` — medium confidence. This is the Payoff
  beat's job: it must answer the *specific* question the Hook asked, not a
  nearby or broader one.
- **Low unique-viewers-with-high-views is a rewatch/loop signal** `[C]` — exactly what
  a mirrored loop is engineered to produce `[C] (Nate Black, IHDJkJpYC90)`.

## Length & retention framing to keep in mind while writing

- **Retention is judged relative to videos of similar topic and length** `[C]` — a
  shorter, tighter video with higher retention can beat a longer one with more
  raw watch time `[C] (vidIQ, SIp7MeYbz8U)`. Don't pad a script to hit a target
  length; a shorter, tighter Short is a legitimate choice, not a compromise.
- **Judge enjoyment by average percentage viewed, not average view duration** `[C]`
  `[C] (Nate Black, IHDJkJpYC90)` — write for a complete, satisfying loop
  rather than for maximum raw seconds.
- **Nate Black: when reviving a dead/random channel, err toward shorter, `[C]`
  punchier videos** `[C] (Nate Black, wqjiXKKqek4)` — a usable default when a
  concept brief doesn't specify a target length: prefer the 20–30s punchy band
  over the 35–45s standard band unless the premise genuinely needs the room.
